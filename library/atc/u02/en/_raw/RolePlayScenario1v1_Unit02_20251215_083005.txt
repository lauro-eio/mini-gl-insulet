SECTION 1: SCENARIO OVERVIEW
• Title: “The Identity Pivot: Transforming Daily Actions into a New Self”
• Purpose: Practice translating small, identity-based actions into sustainable behavioral change through a guided role-play interaction.
• Connection to Unit: This scenario tests the core principle from Unit 02 by having participants articulate and defend an aspirational identity through atomic habits, reinforcing the concept that every daily micro-action is a vote for who you want to become.
• Duration: 30–45 minutes

SECTION 2: ROLES AND CONFLICT
• Role 1: The Transformation Champion
 – Profile: A motivated professional seeking to break the cycle of sporadic, outcome-focused wins by adopting a new self-image based on tiny, consistent wins.
 – Goal: Reinforce a healthy, forward-thinking identity by integrating 1% daily improvements that align with not just what they do, but who they are.
 – Motivation: Frustrated by traditional goal-setting methods, they are determined to prove that identity-based actions can lead to exponential personal and professional growth.
 – Specific Actions: They practice articulating their daily habit-building techniques, use language that reflects self-image transformation (e.g., “I am becoming a disciplined leader”), and propose tangible micro-actions that serve as votes for their new identity.
  
• Role 2: The Skeptical Guardian
 – Profile: A traditional manager or stakeholder, accustomed to outcome-driven performance metrics, who views the micro-improvement approach as too subtle or ineffective.
 – Counter-Goal: Maintain familiar, large-goal oriented strategies focused solely on results, challenging the value of investing in identity shifts over immediate, visible outcomes.
 – Primary Objection: They argue that without dramatic, immediate changes, progress is merely incremental and may not justify time or resources.
 – Resistance Tactic: They question the efficacy of atomic habits with pointed, outcome-centered challenges and ask for concrete evidence that such small actions will truly drive long-term transformation.
  
• Conflict Thesis: The core conflict centers on whether identity-based, micro-improvement strategies can effectively challenge entrenched outcome-focused mindsets, ultimately convincing the Skeptical Guardian to invest in long-term behavioral change.

SECTION 3: FACILITATOR GUIDE
• Setup:
 – Group participants in pairs, assigning one the role of Transformation Champion and the other the role of Skeptical Guardian.
 – Begin with a brief overview of the unit content emphasizing the importance of small, identity-shaping actions and the concept of “each action as a vote for the new identity.”
 – Provide each participant with a role card outlining objectives and talking points.
  
• Task:
 – In the role-play, the Transformation Champion must present a convincing strategy for achieving breakthrough results through daily micro-actions aligned with an aspirational identity.
 – Simultaneously, the Skeptical Guardian challenges the proposed approach by demanding outcomes and evidence that these tiny changes will compound.
 – Participants are instructed to simulate a strategic dialogue, with the Champion using identity-focused language and reflective self-assessments to counter resistance.
  
• Success Metrics:
 1. The Transformation Champion explicitly references how micro-actions serve as “votes” for their new identity.
 2. Clear articulation of the link between daily routines and long-term identity shifts (e.g., “I am becoming someone who embraces incremental growth”).
 3. Effective reframing of the Skeptical Guardian’s outcome-focused objections into identity-based language.
 4. Use of reflective questions and self-assessment techniques to highlight personal growth over time.
 5. Agreement on at least one specific, actionable micro-improvement to be implemented in the coming week.

SECTION 4: DEBRIEF QUESTIONS
• How did shifting the conversation from outcomes to identity change the dynamics of your interaction?
• In what ways did the Transformation Champion successfully use language to reframe small actions as powerful identity votes?
• What challenges did you encounter when countering outcome-focused objections, and how did you overcome them?
• How did body language or tone influence your perceptions of commitment to the new identity strategy?
• What specific micro-action discussed during the role-play can you commit to as your next “vote” towards your aspirational identity, and why?
