SECTION 1: SCENARIO OVERVIEW
• Title: The 1% Breakthrough – Designing Tiny Actions for Exponential Impact  
• Purpose: Practice translating major goals into small, actionable atomic habits within a system that ensures long-term growth.  
• Connection to Unit: This scenario tests the core principles of Unit 01 by having participants break down ambitious goals into manageable steps (1.1 and 1.2) while linking these tiny improvements to a transformative, cumulative process that builds a new identity.  
• Duration: 30–45 minutes

──────────────────────────────
SECTION 2: ROLES AND CONFLICT
• Role 1: The Habit Architect  
 – Profile: A passionate professional determined to revamp their daily routine using the power of 1% improvements.  
 – Goal: To design a robust, step-by-step system that integrates atomic habits into their routine, aligning actions with their aspirational identity.  
 – Motivation: Frustrated by sporadic results and the pitfalls of “all-or-nothing” approaches; they believe that consistent, minor enhancements can unlock sustainable, breakthrough success.  
 – Actions to Practice: Breaking down a significant goal into simple, daily steps; articulating how slight changes aggregate into monumental shifts; and reinforcing the new habit by aligning it with identity change.  

• Role 2: The Skeptical Stakeholder  
 – Profile: A cautious manager or colleague who questions the feasibility and impact of focusing on micro-improvements.  
 – Counter-Goal: To maintain traditional, big-goal-oriented strategies and resist the shift towards a system based on small, incremental changes.  
 – Primary Objection: Argues that dramatic change requires dramatic action and that incremental steps are too slow to yield recognizable results.  
 – Tactic: Uses data skepticism and past failures of small changes, challenging the relevance of atomic habits by focusing on the need for immediate, large-scale impacts.

• Conflict Thesis: The core conflict to resolve is proving that a system built on tiny, consistent actions—when aligned with one’s identity—can overcome short-term resistance and yield exponential, long-term success.

──────────────────────────────
SECTION 3: FACILITATOR GUIDE
• Setup:  
 – Group participants into pairs (or small groups if preferred).  
 – Assign one participant as the Habit Architect and the other(s) as the Skeptical Stakeholder.  
 – Begin with a brief explanation of how atomic improvements work, then let the roles initiate a discussion/debate based on an ambitious goal needing systematization.

• Task:  
 – The Habit Architect must present a structured plan to break down a large goal into daily 1% improvements, connecting these actions to both immediate benefits and long-term identity changes.  
 – The Skeptical Stakeholder must challenge the plan by questioning the feasibility, the timeline, and how meaningful results will emerge from such small steps.  
 – Together, both roles should work toward a resolution by refining the plan to address concerns about sustainability and visible progress.

• Success Metrics:  
 1. The Habit Architect clearly specifies actionable, tiny steps that cumulatively build toward the larger goal.  
 2. The presentation includes direct references to habit loop mechanics (cue, craving, response, reward) and environmental optimization that supports sustainable change.  
 3. The argument integrates an identity shift perspective—articulating how small changes support the transformation into the desired self-image.  
 4. The Skeptical Stakeholder’s concerns are addressed with evidence and real-world examples (e.g., the British Cycling marginal gains concept).  
 5. Both participants display active listening and collaborative problem-solving towards a mutually agreeable, refined plan.

SECTION 4: DEBRIEF QUESTIONS
1. How did adopting the 1% improvement mindset shift your overall approach to breaking down the larger goal?  
2. In what ways did the conversation challenge your preconceptions about the effectiveness of tiny, consistent actions?  
3. How did addressing the Skeptical Stakeholder's counterpoints help in refining the system-based approach for habit development?  
4. What specific aspects of the habit loop (cue, craving, response, reward) did you find most compelling for overcoming resistance?  
5. How can you apply the principles discussed in this role-play to real-life challenges in both your personal and professional arenas?
