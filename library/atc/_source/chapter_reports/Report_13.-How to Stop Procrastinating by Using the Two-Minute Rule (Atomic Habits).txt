Chapter's Primary Thesis
• Procrastination can be overcome by reducing any new habit to an easily executable, two-minute version, thereby establishing a foundation that encourages consistent action and ultimately leads to complex behavior change.

Key Concepts
• Two-Minute Rule – Initiating any new habit with an action that takes two minutes or less.
• Gateway Habit – The minimal starting action that naturally leads to the full habit.
• Decisive Moments – Critical junctures where small choices direct the course of the day.
• Habit Shaping – Gradually improving or expanding a habit through incremental stages.
• Ritualization – The process of turning a small, repeatable act into a regular routine.

Actionable Steps
• Identify the target habit and determine its minimal two-minute version (e.g., “put on running shoes” instead of “go for a run”).
• Map the habit progression from very easy to very hard, establishing clear incremental phases.
• Implement the two-minute version daily to standardize the behavior, building a consistent “show-up” foundation.
• Monitor and gradually extend the habit beyond the initial two minutes once the routine is established.
• Reinforce the new identity by consistently performing the minimal habit, thereby casting votes for the desired behavioral change.

Core Conflict/Problem
• The chapter addresses the common misconception that drastic initial efforts are necessary for behavior change, demonstrating how large goals often lead to procrastination due to perceived difficulty and resistance.

Sequence Summary
• Follows previous discussions on making habits obvious and attractive by shifting focus to ease of initiation.
• Transitions from establishing habit cues and motivations into practical, manageable actions.
• Sets up subsequent content on habit satisfaction and long-term adherence by establishing a reliable framework for consistent action starting with simplified steps.
