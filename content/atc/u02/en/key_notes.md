# Key Notes — Three Layers, One Identity

**Unit goal:** Help frontline leaders build team identity so daily actions reinforce who the crew is becoming—not only what they hit this week.

## Core ideas

- **Three layers of change:** Outcome (the result), process (the steps), identity (the belief underneath both). Coach all three, not only the first two.
- **Votes for identity:** Every completed micro-action is evidence the team submits to itself about who it is—each repetition is a small vote for "we are a reliable team."
- **Identity must be attainable today:** A statement like "we are the pod that catches errors" only motivates if there is an obvious, doable action this shift proves it.
- **Habits become self-defining:** A behavior repeated long enough in a stable context stops feeling like a rule and starts feeling like identity—which is why it survives when the supervisor is off-shift.
- **Contradictions break identity fast:** A padded number or a skipped ritual under pressure damages the team's self-story faster than a missed target ever will.

## Frontline leader checklist

1. Write one aspirational identity sentence for your team: "We are the kind of crew that ________."
2. Pick one small, visible action this shift that would count as evidence for that sentence.
3. End stand-up or handover with "who were we today"—one honest sentence, said out loud.
4. Watch for a moment this week when the identity is tested (rush order, new hire, bad shift) and coach the team through protecting it.
5. Ask one direct report to narrate the identity to a newer teammate, so it does not depend only on your voice.

## Reflection

If a new hire joined your team tomorrow with no orientation document, what would they learn about "who we are" just from watching one shift?
