# Role-Play 1v1 — Defending an Identity Ritual to a Skeptical Peer

**Duration:** 25–35 minutes  
**Purpose:** Practice defending a team identity ritual against a peer who thinks it is soft, slow, or unnecessary once numbers look fine.

## Roles

**Role 1 — Frontline supervisor (Identity Coach)**  
Your team has an identity ritual (a self-check, a handover phrase, a stand-up line) that is starting to slip because the scoreboard still looks acceptable. You must connect the ritual to a concrete outcome, not just a feeling.

**Role 2 — Skeptical former peer**  
You respect the supervisor but think the ritual is "extra talk" that slows the shift down. Numbers are fine, so you push to drop or shorten it under time pressure. You will yield only if shown a specific moment where the ritual already caught something the number would have missed.

## Conflict

Can the supervisor protect the identity ritual without sounding like they are defending a feeling instead of a result?

## Facilitator notes

- Round 1 (8 min): Peer pushes to cut the ritual under time pressure; coach responds.
- Round 2 (8 min): Coach reframes the ritual using one concrete catch or near-miss it prevented.
- Debrief (10 min): Where did the coach slip into vague identity language with no evidence? What single example would have convinced the skeptic faster?
