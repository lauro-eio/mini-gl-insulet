# Self-Assessment — Are You Coaching Outcomes or Identity?

Answer for how you **actually** lead this month (A=1 … D=4). Be honest—this is diagnostic, not a performance review.

--- SECTION 1: QUESTIONS ---

**Q1.** When your team hits a good number, what do you say in the moment?
- A) Nothing—good numbers don't need comment
- B) "Nice, keep it up" and move on
- C) "That's the process working" and name the step that produced it
- D) "That's who we are now" and tie it to the team's identity sentence

**Q2.** A new hire joins mid-quarter. How do they learn "how we operate here"?
- A) They pick it up randomly from whoever is near them
- B) I give them the onboarding checklist once
- C) A senior teammate walks them through the process steps
- D) They watch the team narrate and defend a shared identity within the first week

**Q3.** Under a rush order, what is most likely to get skipped first?
- A) Whatever slows us down, including quality checks
- B) The extra step, if no one is watching closely
- C) Nothing—the process is fixed regardless of pressure
- D) Nothing—skipping it would contradict who we say we are

**Q4.** How do you talk about a missed target with your team?
- A) I focus only on the number and who is responsible
- B) I mention the number, then move to next week's plan
- C) I connect the miss to a process gap we can fix
- D) I ask what it says about the identity we are building, then fix the process

**Q5.** When a number looks good but you suspect it was inflated to look better than reality, you:
- A) Let it go—green tiles keep leadership calm
- B) Quietly note it but don't raise it
- C) Investigate the process step that let it happen
- D) Name the contradiction openly—it breaks the team's identity story faster than a bad number would

**Q6.** How visible is your team's sense of "who we are" to someone observing one shift?
- A) Invisible—there is no shared story, just individual habits
- B) Vague—people could describe the job, not an identity
- C) Present in the process documents and checklists
- D) Visible in how people talk, correct each other, and coach newcomers without me present

**Q7.** You are out sick for two days. What runs the floor in your absence?
- A) Whoever is loudest that day
- B) The last instructions I left before leaving
- C) The standard process, mostly unchanged
- D) The team's own sense of who it is, defended without my prompting

**Q8.** Success for you as a frontline leader means:
- A) The scoreboard looks good this week
- B) The scoreboard looks good and no one complains
- C) The team follows a reliable process consistently
- D) The team has become people who would choose the right action even off the clock

--- SECTION 2: SCORING ---

Score A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4. Total range 8–32.

--- SECTION 3: PROFILES ---

1. **Scoreboard Manager (8–14):** You coach the result and little else. Numbers move week to week with no anchor underneath them. Pick one process this month and name the identity it proves.
2. **Process Coach (15–20):** You have solid steps and checklists, but the team rarely talks about who it is becoming. Add one identity sentence to your next stand-up.
3. **Identity Builder (21–26):** You already connect actions to a team story. Strengthen it by rehearsing what happens when the identity is tested under pressure.
4. **Identity Anchor (27–32):** Your team runs on a shared identity that survives your absence. Teach one direct report to narrate and defend it, so it outlasts any single leader.
