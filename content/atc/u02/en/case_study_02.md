# Case 2 — Two Pods, Same Metric, Different Identity

## Opening

Two customer-support pods sit on the same floor, tracked on the same headline metric: first-contact resolution. Pod A, led by Tomás, hit strong numbers for two straight months. Pod B, led by Ana, hit slightly lower numbers but the gap kept narrowing. Leadership planned to hold up Tomás's pod as the model for a company town hall.

## Context

A closer look, prompted by an unrelated quality audit, showed the two pods got their numbers in very different ways. Tomás's team, under pressure to keep the streak alive, had started closing tickets as "resolved" after a single reply even when the customer's issue was still open—technically hitting the metric, not actually solving the problem. No one on the team objected out loud, but two agents privately told the auditor they felt uneasy about it and stopped correcting each other on borderline calls.

Ana's team resolves fewer tickets on the first contact, but agents openly coach each other mid-shift: "would you close this if it were your own account?" That question, repeated for weeks, had become something people said unprompted, even to Ana herself when she was rushing.

## Decision point

The audit lands on Ana's manager's desk the same week as the town-hall planning email. She can:

1. Quietly ask Tomás to tighten reporting and still feature his pod, since the public number is what leadership remembers.
2. Feature Ana's pod instead and use the audit finding to explain why a slower, honest number matters more than a fast one.
3. Cancel the town-hall spotlight entirely and treat both numbers as equally unreliable until further review.

## Exhibits (simplified)

| Week | Pod A first-contact resolution | Pod B first-contact resolution | Reopen rate (either pod) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 71% | 66% | 9% |
| 2 | 74% | 68% | 8% |
| 3 | 78% | 70% | 7% |
| 4 (audit) | 80% | 72% | Pod A: 21% · Pod B: 6% |

## Discussion questions

1. What does the reopen-rate column reveal that the headline metric hides—and why is that gap an identity problem, not only a measurement problem?
2. How should Ana's manager talk to Tomás's pod without shaming individual agents who stayed quiet under pressure?
3. Write the identity sentence Ana's pod has actually earned, based on what people say to each other mid-shift—not based on the scoreboard.
