# Strategic Role-Play — Protecting Team Identity Under Pressure

**Duration:** 40–50 minutes  
**Purpose:** Practice protecting a team's identity ritual when a rush order or shortcut request would quietly break it.

## Four roles

1. **Supervisor (protagonist):** Wants to keep the identity ritual running through a rush order without slowing the shift enough to draw complaints.
2. **Senior ops lead (antagonist):** Wants the rush order cleared fast and sees the ritual as a step that can be skipped "just this once."
3. **Quality analyst (skeptic):** Needs evidence the ritual actually prevents defects or rework, not just team morale, before defending it upward.
4. **Peer supervisor (ally or swing):** Will support keeping the ritual only if it does not create an unfair double standard against their own pod.

## Conflict thesis

Can the supervisor protect the identity ritual during real pressure without looking inflexible—and without letting "just this once" become the new normal?

## Success look-fors

- One concrete link between the ritual and a defect, rework, or reopen it prevents
- A clear line on what does not get skipped even under a rush order
- A fair standard that does not single out one pod
- No quiet second version of the process that contradicts the public one

## Debrief prompts

1. Which argument moved the ops lead—identity language or outcome language?
2. What would it have cost the team's identity if the ritual had been skipped "just this once"?
3. How does the supervisor make sure this exception does not become next week's default?
