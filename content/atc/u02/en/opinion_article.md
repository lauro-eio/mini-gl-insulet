# The Day My Team Stopped Waiting for Me to Say Good Job

For two years I ran my pod on praise. Green tile, I said "great work." Red tile, I said "let's push harder." I thought I was coaching. I was actually the only battery in the room—the moment I stepped out for a dentist appointment, the praise stopped, and so did the effort.

The turn came from an unlikely place: a new hire named Priya asked me why we double-checked outbound labels when the system already flagged errors. I gave her the process answer—fewer returns, cleaner audit. She nodded, unconvinced. A week later I heard her tell another new hire something different: "We're the crew that doesn't let a mistake leave this station. That's just who we are here." I hadn't taught her that line. She had absorbed it from watching us do the check, shift after shift, long before I ever said it out loud.

That is when I understood I had been coaching the wrong layer. I was managing the scoreboard and the checklist, but never naming the identity underneath them. So I stopped ending stand-up with "let's hit the number" and started ending it with "who were we today"—one sentence, no more than that. Some days the answer was small: "we caught a mislabel before it left the dock." Some days it was blunt: "we let one slide because we were rushing." Either way, we said it out loud, together, every shift.

Three weeks in, I was out for two days with a family emergency. I came back expecting the usual drift. Instead, the whiteboard had a note from Priya: "we caught 4/4 this week—still that crew." Nobody had told her to write it. The identity had become something the team defended on its own, not something I had to police.

I am not naive about this. A slogan on a wall does nothing. What changed my floor was smaller and slower: one visible action every shift that gave the team evidence for who it was becoming, repeated long enough that the story started writing itself. If you supervise people who used to stand beside you on the line, do not just hand them a checklist. Give them a sentence they can say about themselves that the checklist proves true. That sentence will still be running the floor after you have gone home.
