# Case 1 — Priya and the Escalation-Avoidance Loop

## Opening

Priya supervises eleven agents on a customer-support floor. Whenever the queue crosses twenty open tickets, she has noticed the same pattern: at least one agent closes a difficult case with a thin, vague note instead of the documented fix. She used to call it "cutting corners." After a training on habit loops, she wants to name what is actually happening before she corrects anyone again.

## Context

The cue is consistent: queue count crossing twenty visible on the shared dashboard. Talking with two agents privately, Priya hears the same craving in different words—relief from being seen as the reason the queue is red. The response, a thin close, delivers that relief fast: the ticket count drops and no one asks questions. The reward reinforces itself before any manager notices the quality problem, which usually surfaces days later as a reopened ticket from an annoyed customer.

Priya's prior fix was a stricter closing-note policy with a warning for violations. It worked for four or five days after each retraining, then quietly reappeared once the queue spiked again. Agents did not see it as breaking rules—they saw it as surviving a bad ten minutes.

## Decision point

The queue is about to spike again this afternoon. Priya can:

1. Reissue the closing-note policy with a firmer warning and monitor closely today.
2. Keep the cue (queue past twenty) and the craving (relief from looking like the problem) the same, but design a new response—flag the ticket in a shared channel for a fast second pass—paired with a visible, quick reward.
3. Quietly reassign difficult tickets away from agents who have done this before, avoiding the conversation entirely.

## Discussion questions

1. What was the original loop actually rewarding, and why did the policy alone fail to remove it?
2. How should Priya introduce the new response so agents experience it as relief, not extra scrutiny?
3. Design the full redesigned loop (cue, craving, response, reward) for this floor, and name who confirms the reward happens within ten minutes.
