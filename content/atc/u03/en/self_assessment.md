# Self-Assessment — How Do You Handle Bad Loops on Your Floor?

Answer for how you **actually** handle repeat behaviors this month (A=1 … D=4). Be honest—this is diagnostic, not a performance review.

--- SECTION 1: QUESTIONS ---

**Q1.** When the same unwanted behavior shows up on your team for the third time, your first move is usually to:
- A) Repeat the rule, louder this time
- B) Give a warning and hope it sticks
- C) Ask what triggers the behavior before reacting
- D) Map the full loop—cue, craving, response, reward—before acting

**Q2.** When you try to identify what "triggers" a bad behavior, you typically:
- A) Blame attitude or lack of effort
- B) Notice the moment but don't name it precisely
- C) Point to a specific, observable cue (a number, a call type, a time of shift)
- D) Track the cue across shifts and confirm it's consistent

**Q3.** When you think about *why* someone repeats a shortcut, you mostly consider:
- A) They are being lazy or careless
- B) They probably forgot the process
- C) The shortcut probably relieves some pressure they feel in the moment
- D) I can usually name the exact craving the shortcut satisfies

**Q4.** Your standard fix for an unwanted response is to:
- A) Ban it and escalate consequences
- B) Remind people of the correct way, again
- C) Offer an alternative response that still meets the need
- D) Redesign the response to the same cue and attach a fast, visible reward

**Q5.** After you introduce a new "correct" behavior, you usually:
- A) Assume it's fixed and move on
- B) Check in occasionally if you remember
- C) Watch for the old behavior returning under the original trigger
- D) Expect one relapse and have a plan ready for when it happens

**Q6.** When a peer or direct report resists your correction, you tend to:
- A) Pull rank and repeat the instruction
- B) Soften the message but keep the same ask
- C) Ask what the current behavior is doing *for* them
- D) Co-design the new response with them so they own the redesign

**Q7.** Rewards for good behavior on your floor mostly happen:
- A) Rarely—compliance is just expected
- B) Occasionally, if I remember to mention it
- C) Consistently, but sometimes with a delay
- D) Immediately and visibly, tied to the specific new response

**Q8.** Success in changing a team habit, for you, means:
- A) The behavior stopped while I was watching
- B) Fewer complaints reach me about it
- C) The new behavior holds for a few weeks
- D) The team runs the new loop correctly without me present

--- SECTION 2: SCORING ---

Score A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4. Total range 8–32.

--- SECTION 3: PROFILES ---

1. **Rule-Repeater (8–14):** You treat repeat behavior as a compliance failure and respond with volume, not diagnosis. Try naming one cue and one craving before your next correction.
2. **Cue-Spotter (15–20):** You notice triggers but still address only the response. Practice naming the craving out loud before you redesign anything.
3. **Loop-Mapper (21–26):** You already map cue, craving, and response for real problems. Strengthen the reward side—make it faster and more visible.
4. **Loop Architect (27–32):** You redesign loops end-to-end and expect relapse as data. Teach one direct report to map a loop themselves this month.
