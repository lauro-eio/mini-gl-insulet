# Self-Assessment — How Do You Drive Change on Your Team?

Answer for how you **actually** lead this month (A=1 … D=4). Be honest—this is diagnostic, not a performance review.

--- SECTION 1: QUESTIONS ---

**Q1.** When your team misses a weekly target, your first move is usually to:
- A) Announce a bold new push for next week
- B) Ask for a few big fixes when people have bandwidth
- C) Break the miss into small process gaps and pick one to fix
- D) Install a tiny daily ritual and track it for two weeks

**Q2.** In 1:1s with a former peer you now supervise, you tend to:
- A) Push a dramatic turnaround plan
- B) Give occasional tips when things feel off
- C) Agree on one small behavior to practice before next week
- D) Co-design a habit loop (cue, action, reward) they own

**Q3.** How do you treat “compounding” team improvements?
- A) Skeptical—too slow for my boss’s timeline
- B) Useful sometimes, but I still prefer splashy wins
- C) I believe small daily gains add up if we stay consistent
- D) I run the team on visible micro-habits and review them weekly

**Q4.** When planning the week, you mainly:
- A) Set one overwhelming outcome for everyone
- B) List several goals and hope capacity appears
- C) Split big work into manageable steps with owners
- D) Design shift-level habits that make the goal almost automatic

**Q5.** After a rough shift, you usually:
- A) Reset everything Monday with a new mandate
- B) Wait for inspiration or a crisis to force change
- C) Look for one friction to remove tomorrow
- D) Reinforce the existing ritual and adjust the cue if needed

**Q6.** Environment design on your floor looks like:
- A) Unchanged—people should just try harder
- B) Occasional posters or reminders
- C) A few cues that support better habits
- D) Tools, boards, and defaults arranged so the right action is easiest

**Q7.** When upper management wants a “big leap,” you:
- A) Pass the pressure straight to the team
- B) Soften it but keep the leap framing
- C) Translate it into a sequence of small, trackable steps
- D) Protect the team’s system and negotiate leading indicators upstairs

**Q8.** Success for you as a frontline leader means:
- A) Dramatic visible saves
- B) Occasional lucky breaks
- C) Steady improvement week to week
- D) A team that runs reliable habits without you hovering

--- SECTION 2: SCORING ---

Score A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4. Total range 8–32.

--- SECTION 3: PROFILES ---

1. **Leap-First Supervisor (8–14):** You default to big resets. Useful in true emergencies; costly as a weekly style. Try one micro-habit for 10 workdays before announcing another leap.
2. **Emerging Builder (15–20):** You see the value of small steps but still chase splashy wins. Lock one ritual to a shift cue and review it every Friday.
3. **Steady Coach (21–26):** You already break goals into practice. Strengthen visibility—make the habit public on the board so peers reinforce it.
4. **Systems Leader (27–32):** You lead through compounding routines. Teach one direct report to own a cue this month so the system survives your absence.
