# Strategic Role-Play — Redesigning the Floor Under Budget Pressure

**Duration:** 40–50 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice securing approval for a low-cost environment redesign when stakeholders default to training or signage instead.

## Four roles

1. **Supervisor (protagonist):** Wants approval to redesign one cue in the physical layout, backed by the team's habits scorecard.
2. **Budget-conscious manager (antagonist):** Prefers a cheap refresher training or a new sign over any physical change; calls redesign "a facilities request, not a leadership fix."
3. **Safety/ops analyst (skeptic):** Wants evidence the cue, not the person, is causing the harmful habit, and a way to check the fix worked.
4. **Peer supervisor (ally or swing):** Will support if the redesign does not create a new bottleneck or cost for their own team.

## Conflict thesis

Can the supervisor win approval for redesigning the environment—not just training the people in it—without sounding like they are avoiding accountability?

## Success look-fors

- A named cue and its evidence (near-misses, scorecard entries, repeat pattern)
- A low-cost first move, distinct from a bigger budget ask
- A way to check whether the redesign changed the habit
- No secret plan to just run more training instead

## Debrief prompts

1. Which argument moved the budget-conscious manager: the scorecard, the cost comparison, or the safety framing?
2. How did the supervisor separate "the room caused this" from "nobody is responsible"?
3. What would the analyst need to see in two weeks to call the redesign a success?
