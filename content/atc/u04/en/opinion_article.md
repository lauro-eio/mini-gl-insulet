# The Day I Stopped Blaming My Team and Moved the Whiteboard

For three weeks I wrote the same note in my shift log: "Team skipping the lane check at the corner by receiving. Reminded again." I said it in stand-up. I put up a sign. Nothing changed, and I started assuming my people just did not care enough.

Then a peer supervisor asked me a question I did not like: "What's actually sitting at that corner?" I walked over. A pallet jack was parked exactly where the lane check should happen, blocking the sightline and making the "quick squeeze-through" the easiest path. My sign was taped to a pillar nobody faced while walking that route. I had been asking people to fight the room and calling it a discipline problem.

I moved the jack's parking spot ten feet away, taped a bright strip on the floor at the exact point where the check should happen, and asked one operator to call out "clear" out loud every time she did it—not for me, just so the moment became visible to herself and to whoever was near. Within two shifts, three other people were saying "clear" without being asked. Nobody got a lecture.

I still track the habit—not with a dramatic new metric, just a tally mark on a card at the corner: checked or skipped. Some days it dips when we're short-staffed and I move fast to fix the layout again, not the person.

If your team keeps missing the same step, look at the floor before you look at the roster. The room is often the loudest thing coaching your people, and most of what it teaches, it teaches without saying a word.
