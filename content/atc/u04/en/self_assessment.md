# Self-Assessment — How Well Do You See Your Team's Cues?

Answer for how you **actually** lead this month (A=1 … D=4). Be honest—this is diagnostic, not a performance review.

--- SECTION 1: QUESTIONS ---

**Q1.** When the same mistake repeats on your floor, your first instinct is to:
- A) Remind people to pay more attention next time
- B) Watch for a while and hope it self-corrects
- C) Ask what in the layout or routine keeps triggering it
- D) Name the exact cue and redesign the spot where it fires

**Q2.** Your habits scorecard for the team (helpful vs. harmful floor habits) is:
- A) Something I have never written down
- B) In my head, not shared with anyone
- C) Drafted once, rarely revisited
- D) Visible, dated, and reviewed on a set cadence

**Q3.** When you notice a harmful habit forming, you tend to:
- A) Address it only after it causes a visible problem
- B) Mention it in passing during a busy shift
- C) Point it out and ask the person to try harder
- D) Trace it to its cue and change something in the environment

**Q4.** "Choice architecture" on your floor—defaults, placement, order of steps—is:
- A) Whatever it has always been; not something I touch
- B) Adjusted occasionally when something breaks
- C) Reviewed when I notice friction
- D) Actively designed so the safe or correct action is the easiest one

**Q5.** When you ask a team member to call out a risky moment out loud, you:
- A) Have never asked anyone to do this
- B) Suggested it once and let it drop
- C) Ask for it during high-risk tasks only
- D) Built it into the normal rhythm of the shift, modeled by you first

**Q6.** Tools, signage, and shared items on your floor are placed:
- A) Wherever they ended up; nobody has revisited it
- B) Roughly where they've always been
- C) Adjusted after a specific complaint or near-miss
- D) Deliberately positioned so the intended habit is the path of least effort

**Q7.** When a harmful habit is really a layout problem, but fixing it costs time or budget, you:
- A) Ask the team to just be more careful instead
- B) Flag it upward and wait
- C) Make a low-cost temporary fix yourself
- D) Redesign what you can now and build a case for the rest

**Q8.** Success in managing team habits, for you, looks like:
- A) Fewer complaints reaching my manager
- B) The team not getting called out for the same thing twice in a row
- C) A shrinking list of harmful habits on the scorecard
- D) An environment that keeps producing good habits even when you're not watching

--- SECTION 2: SCORING ---

Score A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4. Total range 8–32.

--- SECTION 3: PROFILES ---

1. **Reminder-First Supervisor (8–14):** You default to asking people to try harder. Start a habits scorecard this week and pick one cue to redesign before adding another reminder.
2. **Noticing Coach (15–20):** You are starting to see cues but rarely change the room itself. Choose one harmful habit and move, relabel, or remove the thing triggering it.
3. **Cue-Aware Leader (21–26):** You already trace habits to their triggers. Make the scorecard visible to the team so they help you spot the next cue.
4. **Environment Architect (27–32):** You lead by redesigning the room, not by repeating instructions. Teach one direct report to run the scorecard so the system survives your absence.
