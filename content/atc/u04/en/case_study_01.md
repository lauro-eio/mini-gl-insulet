# Case 1 — Marta and the Blind-Corner Habit

## Opening

Marta supervises a receiving crew at a regional distribution center. For a month, operators have been "squeezing through" a blind corner near the dock instead of stopping for the required lane check. No incident yet, but two near-misses were logged. Marta has reminded the crew four times in stand-up. The habit persists.

## Context

The corner in question has a parked pallet jack that blocks the sightline and narrows the walkway, which makes the quick squeeze-through the physically easiest option. The lane-check sign is taped to a pillar that faces away from the walking path. Marta's manager, Devon, wants the near-misses at zero and has asked for "a refresher training" for the whole shift.

Marta suspects training will not fix a habit that the layout itself is rewarding. She has never run a habits scorecard for her crew—problems get handled one complaint at a time, not tracked as a pattern.

## Decision point

Devon wants the refresher scheduled for Friday. Marta can:

1. Run the refresher training as requested and consider the issue closed.
2. Start a habits scorecard, name the exact cue (jack placement, sign location, walkway width), and redesign the corner before training anyone.
3. Do both: a short, honest stand-up about the real cause, paired with an immediate low-cost environment fix, and propose the scorecard as an ongoing practice.

## Discussion questions

1. What is the blind corner "teaching" the crew, regardless of what Marta says in stand-up?
2. How should Marta talk to Devon so redesigning the environment does not sound like she is dodging accountability for training?
3. Design the habits scorecard entry for this habit: is it helpful or harmful, what is the cue, and what single environment change would you make first?
