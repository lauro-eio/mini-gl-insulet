# Key Notes — See the Cue, Score the Habit, Redesign the Room

**Unit goal:** Notice the cues that trigger team habits, score which floor habits help or hurt, and redesign the environment so good habits stick without constant willpower.

## Core ideas

- **Cues run habits:** most repeated behavior is triggered by something already in the room—layout, tool placement, a face, a sound—not a fresh decision each time.
- **Make it conscious:** naming a cue out loud, right when it appears, turns an automatic moment into a choice for one critical second.
- **Habits scorecard:** list current floor and team habits and mark each one helpful or harmful before trying to change anything.
- **Choice architecture:** the easiest path wins. Change the default, the distance, or the visibility, and behavior shifts with less friction than a new rule.

## Frontline leader checklist

1. Walk your floor and list five recurring habits—yours and the team's. Mark each helpful (H) or harmful (X).
2. For one harmful habit, name the exact cue that triggers it (place, time, object, person).
3. Change one piece of the environment—move it, remove it, or relabel it—so the helpful response is the easy one.
4. Pick one moment where a cue should be called out loud, and ask one person to practice it this week.
5. Make the scorecard visible (board, shift card, checklist)—not just something in your head.
6. Schedule a 10-minute review: which habit moved, which cue still needs a redesign.

## Reflection

Which harmful habit on your floor is being caused by the room itself, not by the people in it?
