# Role-Play 1v1 — Defending the Peer Check-In to a Veteran Who Calls It Soft

**Duration:** 25–35 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice defending a standing peer check-in after a near-miss or incident, without backing down under pressure from a respected, skeptical veteran.

## Roles

**Role 1 — Frontline supervisor (Practice Owner)**
You want to make a brief, opt-in peer check-in standard practice after any near-miss or incident on your team—not a formal debrief, just a private, low-key conversation within a day or two. You must not let the practice get watered down into "just tell HR" or dropped entirely to avoid friction.

**Role 2 — Skeptical veteran teammate**
You have worked the floor for fifteen years without anything like this and see it as unnecessary hand-holding that implies the crew cannot handle their own job. You will back off only if the supervisor can explain, concretely, what this adds beyond the existing incident report and safety briefing.

## Conflict

Can the Practice Owner secure the veteran's buy-in—or at least non-interference—for the peer check-in without it being dismissed as soft, or quietly undermined once the supervisor is not watching?

## Facilitator notes

- Round 1 (8 min): Veteran challenges the practice as unnecessary; Practice Owner responds.
- Round 2 (8 min): Practice Owner distinguishes the peer check-in from the formal incident report and proposes how it would actually run.
- Debrief (10 min): Where did the Practice Owner nearly concede too much? What language actually landed with the veteran?
