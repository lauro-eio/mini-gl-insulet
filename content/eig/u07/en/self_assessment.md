# Self-Assessment — Do You Train the Skill or Embed It?

Answer for how you **actually** lead this month (A=1 … D=4). Be honest—this is diagnostic, not a performance review.

--- SECTION 1: QUESTIONS ---

**Q1.** When you onboard a new hire, teaching them what to do with a sudden spike of stress or fear is:
- A) Not something you plan for; they pick it up eventually like everyone else
- B) Something you mention once if it comes up naturally
- C) Something you build into their first week deliberately
- D) Something a buddy actively checks in on with them for the first two weeks

**Q2.** In your pre-shift huddle, naming your own emotional reaction to something that happened recently is:
- A) Something you would never do; it is not what huddles are for
- B) Something you might mention in passing, rarely
- C) Something you do occasionally to make a point
- D) A standing part of how you run the huddle, at least weekly

**Q3.** After a near-miss or incident, your team's practice for checking on the people involved is:
- A) There isn't one; people are expected to move on
- B) Informal—whoever happens to ask, asks
- C) You personally check in, but it depends on you remembering
- D) A standing, opt-in peer check happens within a day or two, regardless of who is on shift

**Q4.** When a new hire visibly freezes or overreacts to a stressful moment early on, you interpret this as:
- A) A sign they are not cut out for the floor
- B) Something they will grow out of with more time on the job
- C) A gap in what you specifically taught them, worth addressing directly
- D) A predictable result of onboarding that skipped the emotional side, and a cue to fix your process, not just this hire

**Q5.** If you were off-shift for a month, would the onboarding buddy check-in, huddle modeling, and peer check still happen?
- A) None of them exist to begin with
- B) Maybe one, inconsistently
- C) Most would continue, but would likely fade without you noticing
- D) All three have a named owner and would run the same without you

**Q6.** Your team's comfort with a teammate naming "that hit me harder than it should have" out loud is:
- A) It would be seen as oversharing or a weakness
- B) It happens occasionally, without much reaction either way
- C) It is generally accepted, especially among experienced staff
- D) It is expected and modeled by leadership, including new hires

**Q7.** When you evaluate whether your onboarding, huddle, or peer-check practice is actually working, you:
- A) Do not evaluate it; you assume it is fine if nobody complains
- B) Notice problems only when something goes visibly wrong
- C) Check in occasionally on whether the practice is still happening
- D) Review each touchpoint on a set schedule and adjust based on what you see

**Q8.** Your approach to building any of these three touchpoints started because:
- A) You have not built any of them yet
- B) Something went wrong and you reacted after the fact
- C) You saw the value and added one deliberately
- D) You treat all three as a standing part of how the team runs, reviewed and maintained like any other process

--- SECTION 2: SCORING ---

Score A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4. Total range 8–32.

--- SECTION 3: PROFILES ---

1. **Task-Only Trainer (8–14):** Onboarding, huddles, and peer support run on autopilot for the technical side only; the emotional half of the job is left for people to figure out alone. Pick one touchpoint—most likely onboarding—and add a single deliberate check-in this month.
2. **Occasional Builder (15–20):** You add these practices when something prompts you to, but they are not standing parts of how the team runs and often depend on you personally. Name one owner for one touchpoint so it survives your next day off.
3. **Deliberate Embedder (21–26):** You have built at least two of the three touchpoints on purpose and check on them periodically. Strengthen the third and set a fixed review cadence for all three.
4. **Systems Owner (27–32):** All three touchpoints run as standing, owned practices that would survive your absence. Teach another leader on your shift how you review and adjust them, so the system outlives your own tenure too.
