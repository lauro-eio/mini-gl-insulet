# Strategic Role-Play — Securing Buy-In to Embed the Check-In Into Onboarding and Huddles

**Duration:** 40–50 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice securing approval to build a check-in practice into onboarding, huddle coaching, and peer support when multiple stakeholders have different priorities.

## Four roles

1. **Supervisor (protagonist):** Wants approval to formally build a two-minute check-in into new-hire onboarding, weekly huddle modeling, and a standing peer check after any incident.
2. **Throughput-focused ops manager (antagonist):** Measured on output and ramp-up speed for new hires; sees any of these as time taken away from task training.
3. **Safety or HR analyst (skeptic):** Supports the idea in principle but wants a concrete owner, a fixed time cost, and a way to tell whether it is actually happening before signing off.
4. **Peer supervisor (ally or swing):** Will support the practice if it does not slow down onboarding on their own crew, and if it does not become another checklist item nobody actually follows.

## Conflict thesis

Can the supervisor secure approval to embed all three touchpoints without losing the argument on speed to the ops manager, or losing credibility on follow-through with the analyst?

## Success look-fors

- A concrete, minimal time cost named for each touchpoint (not open-ended)
- A named owner for each touchpoint, including a backup
- A simple way to check whether each touchpoint is actually happening, not just approved on paper
- No agreement to build all three at once if that risks none of them sticking

## Debrief prompts

1. Which argument landed with the ops manager versus the analyst?
2. How did the supervisor turn "two-minute check-in" into language that reads as protecting performance, not adding overhead?
3. What would cause one of the three touchpoints to quietly disappear first under the next crunch, and who owns preventing that?
