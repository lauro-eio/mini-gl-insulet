# Key Notes — Building the Skill Into the Job, Not Just the Workshop

**Unit goal:** Embed the team's emotional-literacy skills—noticing a surge, naming it, choosing a response—into three touchpoints that outlast a single workshop: new-hire onboarding, pre-shift huddle coaching, and peer check-ins after an incident.

## Core ideas

- **A skill learned once fades:** what a session teaches does not automatically transfer to the job or survive over time without reinforcement built into the work environment afterward (Baldwin & Ford, 1988).
- **The strongest predictors are outside the training room:** a supportive environment, real opportunity to practice, and deliberate follow-up matter more than how the session itself was delivered (Grossman & Salas, 2011; Salas et al., 2012).
- **Onboarding sets habits that are hard to unwind later:** new hires who only get task and compliance training, without culture and connection, adjust more slowly and are more likely to leave (Bauer, 2010).
- **The skill only survives if it is safe to say out loud:** teams with higher psychological safety show more of the small learning behaviors—naming a concern, flagging an error—that let a skill compound (Edmondson, 1999).
- **Peer-delivered check-ins are a low-risk way to keep a team talking:** a brief, non-clinical peer conversation after a hard moment showed no evidence of harm and modestly better team functioning in a controlled trial (Greenberg et al., 2010).

## Frontline leader checklist

1. Build a short check-in into every new hire's first two weeks—not just a machine or task checklist.
2. Model naming a recent surge yourself in the huddle at least once a week, so it reads as normal, not remedial.
3. Set a standing, opt-in peer check after any near-miss or incident, timed within a day or two.
4. Name an owner for each of the three touchpoints so the practice does not depend on you being on shift.
5. Review one touchpoint a month: did it actually happen, or did it quietly stop when the schedule got tight?

## Reflection

Which of the three touchpoints—onboarding, huddle, or peer check—is most likely to quietly disappear on your team under the next deadline, and what would it take to protect it?
