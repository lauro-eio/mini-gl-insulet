# Role-Play 1v1 — Defending a Named-State Pause to a Peer Who Wants Faster Feedback

**Duration:** 25–35 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice protecting a brief state check before a high-stakes coaching conversation without freezing the schedule or losing a peer's trust.

## Roles

**Role 1 — Frontline supervisor (State Owner)**
You are about to coach a direct report on a real issue, but you know you are carrying a feeling from something else that happened earlier today. You want two to three minutes to name the specific feeling and check whether it belongs to this report before you start. You must not skip the check just to stay on schedule, and you must not turn it into an open-ended delay.

**Role 2 — Skeptical peer supervisor**
You are also behind on your own coaching schedule and believe feedback should happen the moment an issue is noticed—"say it now, keep moving." You see the pause as overthinking your own feelings instead of just doing the job. You will back off only if the State Owner names the specific trigger and gives a concrete, short time box for the check.

## Conflict

Can the State Owner secure a short delay for one specific coaching conversation without the peer escalating over their head or treating it as an excuse to avoid a hard conversation?

## Facilitator notes

- Round 1 (8 min): Peer pushes for immediate feedback; State Owner responds.
- Round 2 (8 min): State Owner names the specific trigger (carryover, record attached, or blurred target) and proposes a time-boxed check.
- Debrief (10 min): Where did the State Owner nearly fold under schedule pressure? What language actually landed with the skeptical peer?
