# Case 1 — Marcus and the Missed-Deadline Coaching Conversation

## Opening

Marcus supervises an assembly cell of six. Owen, one of his most experienced techs, has missed his end-of-shift target for the third week running. Marcus has a 1:1 booked with him in fifteen minutes to address it directly.

## Context

Twenty minutes before the 1:1, Marcus's own manager pulled him aside and criticized him, in front of a peer supervisor, for a maintenance request that took two days longer than promised—something entirely outside Marcus's control. He is still carrying the sting of that exchange when Owen sits down across from him. He notices his jaw is tight and his opening line already forming: "This is the third week in a row, and I need it to stop." He has maybe five seconds before he says it.

Owen, for his part, has no idea any of this happened. He is expecting a conversation about his own numbers.

## Decision point

Marcus can:

1. Open with the line as it stands—"third week in a row, I need it to stop"—and let the conversation proceed with whatever tone comes out.
2. Take five seconds, silently name what he is actually feeling ("residual anger at my manager, not at Owen"), then open by asking Owen what has been different about his shifts the past three weeks.
3. Reschedule the 1:1 for later in the day, telling Owen only that something came up, without naming why to himself or preparing differently for the rescheduled conversation.

## Discussion questions

1. What in Marcus's body told him the feeling walking into the room did not belong to Owen, and how could he use that signal instead of letting it drive his opening line?
2. If Marcus chooses option 2, what does he actually say in the first ten seconds, and how does it differ from the line he originally had ready?
3. What would Owen have taken away from the conversation if Marcus had chosen option 1 instead—and would that feedback have been usable to him?
