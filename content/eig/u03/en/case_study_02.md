# Case 2 — Priya and the Repeat Quality Miss

## Opening

Priya leads a packing team of nine at a distribution center. One of her packers, Lena, has had a second quality miss this quarter—a mislabeled shipment that reached a customer. This time, the conversation will be documented and forwarded to HR, since a second miss inside ninety days triggers a formal write-up under company policy.

## Context

Priya likes Lena and has advocated for her before. Sitting down to prepare the documented conversation, Priya realizes she cannot cleanly separate two different feelings: real concern about the mislabeling itself, and a quieter worry that this write-up will make Priya look like she cannot manage her own team, in front of the same manager who reviews her quarterly. She is not sure, honestly, which feeling is going to be driving the room when Lena sits down. The meeting is in ten minutes, and the write-up template is already open on her screen.

## Decision point

Priya can:

1. Proceed with the meeting as scheduled, treating the uncertainty about her own feelings as not relevant since the write-up itself is procedural and required either way.
2. Take a few minutes before the meeting to name both feelings separately—concern about the mislabeling, and worry about how it reflects on her—so she can check which one is actually shaping what she plans to say to Lena.
3. Ask a peer supervisor to sit in and lead the documented portion of the conversation, while Priya stays silent, avoiding the risk that her own mixed feelings show up at all.

## Discussion questions

1. Why does a "record attached" conversation raise the stakes of an unnamed or blurred feeling more than an informal check-in would?
2. If Priya chooses option 2, how might separating the two feelings change what she actually says to Lena about the mislabeling itself?
3. What risk does option 3 avoid, and what does it fail to solve for Priya the next time a documented conversation comes up?
