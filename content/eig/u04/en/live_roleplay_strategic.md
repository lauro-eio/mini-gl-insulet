# Strategic Role-Play — Protecting a Real-Time Regulation Standard Under Deadline Pressure

**Duration:** 40–50 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice securing buy-in for a protected regulation standard when multiple stakeholders want the floor to react and move at full speed.

## Four roles

1. **Supervisor (protagonist):** Wants approval to run a standing rule: any moment with a flooded body, a spiraling mind, or a loaded story gets one named regulation move—breathe, ground, or reframe—before a response.
2. **Speed-focused ops manager (antagonist):** Measured on throughput and cutoff times; sees any pause as lost minutes and calls the standard "getting soft on the floor."
3. **Quality or safety analyst (skeptic):** Supports the idea in principle but needs named criteria for when each move applies, plus a clear time box so it cannot be used to avoid a hard conversation indefinitely.
4. **Peer supervisor (ally or swing):** Will support the standard if it does not slow down moments that genuinely do not need it, and if it does not quietly shift extra tension onto neighboring shifts.

## Conflict thesis

Can the supervisor protect a named, time-boxed regulation move for genuinely high-intensity moments without looking soft to the ops manager or vague to the analyst?

## Success look-fors

- Named moves (breathe, ground, reframe) tied to named triggers, not a blanket "pause whenever you feel something"
- A fixed time box for each move, agreed in advance
- A visible record of when a move was used and what it changed
- No secret second process that contradicts the agreed standard under pressure

## Debrief prompts

1. Which argument landed with the ops manager versus the analyst?
2. How did the supervisor turn "regulation move" into language that reads as risk control, not softness?
3. What would cause the standard to quietly disappear during the next crunch week—and who owns preventing that?
