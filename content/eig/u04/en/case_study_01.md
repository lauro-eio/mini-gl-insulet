# Case 1 — Priya and the Outage on the Pick Floor

## Opening

Priya leads a forty-person pick shift at a distribution center during peak volume. Fifteen minutes into the rush, the scan system drops floor-wide. Screens freeze mid-order, pickers stop moving, and three team leads are already looking at her, waiting for instructions. Priya feels her chest tighten and her thoughts start racing through everything that could go wrong in the next hour.

## Context

Her phone lights up—a customer calling about a stalled order. At the same time, the shift's regional manager pings her for a status update, and a picker near her station asks, loudly enough for four people to hear, "are we getting paid if we're just standing here?" Priya has maybe ten seconds before whatever she says next sets the tone for forty people who are already reading her face.

A team lead quietly mentions the system usually restores within fifteen minutes based on the last two outages—information Priya has not had time to process yet.

## Decision point

Priya can:

1. Answer the customer's call immediately, still flooded, and give a status update to her manager in the same breath—reacting to whichever demand is loudest.
2. Run two slow exhales before responding to anyone, then answer the picker's question first with what she actually knows.
3. Say nothing to the floor, disappear to a back office to think, and address everyone once she has a full plan—leaving forty people without direction in the meantime.

## Discussion questions

1. What told Priya her spike was bigger than the outage itself warranted, and which regulation move—breathe, ground, or reframe—fits that spike best?
2. If Priya chooses option 2, what does she say to the picker in the ten seconds after her second exhale, and how is it different from what she would have said unregulated?
3. Design a floor cue Priya's team could use so everyone can see when she is regulating, without it reading as her losing control of the outage.
