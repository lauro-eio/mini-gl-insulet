# Self-Assessment — Do You Fight the Task or Fight the Person?

Answer for how you **actually** handle floor friction this month (A=1 … D=4). Be honest—this is diagnostic, not a performance review.

--- SECTION 1: QUESTIONS ---

**Q1.** When a peer disagrees with your call on the floor, you usually:
- A) Take it personally and defend yourself
- B) Feel defensive but let the moment pass without addressing it
- C) Separate their point about the work from any tension it stirs up
- D) Name out loud that this is a task disagreement, not a trust issue, before responding

**Q2.** When someone on your crew makes an honest mistake that affects your work, you:
- A) Quietly start wondering whether you can rely on them going forward
- B) Address the mistake but stay privately unsure about their ability
- C) Address the mistake and clearly separate "made an error" from "can't be trusted"
- D) Fix the mistake together and treat it as evidence for their ability, not against it

**Q3.** When you discover a teammate hid or downplayed a mistake instead of reporting it, you:
- A) Let it go without addressing the dishonesty directly
- B) Address it the same way you would address an honest error
- C) Name the difference between a mistake and a mistake that was hidden from you
- D) Name the difference, and expect a track record of transparency—not just an apology—before trust resets

**Q4.** After a public disagreement about a call, your crew usually sees you:
- A) Avoid the other person afterward
- B) Smooth it over without naming what actually happened
- C) Address the task issue openly and let the relationship recover on its own
- D) Actively separate the task issue from the relationship and repair each on purpose

**Q5.** When someone apologizes to you after breaking your trust, what matters most to you is:
- A) Whatever they happen to say in the moment
- B) Whether the apology sounds sincere
- C) Whether the apology matches what actually broke
- D) Whether their behavior afterward proves it, not just the words

**Q6.** Your team's habit around disagreeing about how to do the work is:
- A) Disagreement usually gets read as a personal attack
- B) People disagree quietly, rarely out loud
- C) People can disagree about the work without it becoming personal, most of the time
- D) The team has a visible norm that protects task disagreement as safe and separate from trust

**Q7.** When you make a mistake that costs the team, you:
- A) Hope nobody brings it up
- B) Explain it away
- C) Name it, fix it, and expect that to be enough
- D) Name it, fix it, and expect to prove over the following weeks that it wasn't a pattern

**Q8.** Your sense of when floor friction is "just the work" versus "a trust problem" is:
- A) You don't distinguish—every friction feels the same
- B) You notice sometimes, usually after the tension has already spread
- C) You actively check which one it is before deciding how to respond
- D) You check which one it is, and if it's trust, which specific leg—ability, benevolence, or integrity—broke

--- SECTION 2: SCORING ---

Score A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4. Total range 8–32.

--- SECTION 3: PROFILES ---

1. **Blended Fighter (8–14):** Task disagreement and trust breaks land the same way for you—as personal. Crews around you learn to avoid raising work disagreements at all. Pick one recurring disagreement and practice naming it as "task, not trust" out loud before responding.
2. **Occasional Separator (15–20):** You notice the difference sometimes, usually after tension has already spread through the crew. Build one standing habit: name whether it's task or trust in the first ten seconds, before deciding how to respond.
3. **Deliberate Diagnoser (21–26):** You already separate task from trust and generally match your response. Strengthen it by naming which specific leg—ability, benevolence, integrity—is in question, so your repair lands more precisely.
4. **Trust Steward (27–32):** You run a visible, consistent practice for diagnosing and repairing floor friction, and your crew has learned to trust the process, not just you. Teach one direct report your diagnosis habit so it survives when you are off-shift.
