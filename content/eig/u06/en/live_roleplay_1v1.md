# Role-Play 1v1 — Naming a Task Disagreement Before a Peer Reads It as Distrust

**Duration:** 25–35 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice raising a genuine disagreement about the work without letting a peer read it as a judgment on their ability—and repairing that read if it happens anyway.

## Roles

**Role 1 — Frontline supervisor (Diagnoser)**
You disagree with a peer's call on a scheduling or sequencing decision that affects your crew. You believe the disagreement is entirely about the work, not about the peer's competence, and you need to say so clearly without softening the actual point.

**Role 2 — Peer supervisor (Guarded)**
You had a rough incident recently where a call of yours was second-guessed publicly, and it stung. You now hear any disagreement about your calls as a question about whether you can be trusted to make them. You will only de-escalate if the Diagnoser explicitly separates the task point from any judgment about you.

## Conflict

Can the Diagnoser make the task disagreement clearly and get it heard as a task disagreement—not back off the actual point, and not leave the peer's guardedness unaddressed?

## Facilitator notes

- Round 1 (8 min): Diagnoser raises the task disagreement; Guarded peer reads it personally and pushes back.
- Round 2 (8 min): Diagnoser explicitly names the difference between the task point and any question of trust, and checks which one the peer is actually reacting to.
- Debrief (10 min): Where did the Diagnoser almost soften the real point to avoid the personal read? What specific language separated the two?
