# Role-Play 1v1 — Defending a Pause-and-Label to a Peer Who Wants a Reaction

**Duration:** 25–35 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice protecting a brief pause-and-label on a high-intensity moment without freezing the floor or losing a peer's trust.

## Roles

**Role 1 — Frontline supervisor (Signal Owner)**
You have felt a strong emotional surge—anger, fear, frustration—tied to a call the team is watching. You want ten to thirty seconds to name the feeling and check what actually happened before you respond. You must not skip the beat just to avoid friction, and you must not turn it into an open-ended delay.

**Role 2 — Skeptical peer supervisor**
You are under the same pressure and believe reactions on the floor should be immediate—"say what you see, move on." You see the pause as overthinking or oversharing feelings on the job. You will back off only if the Signal Owner names the specific trigger and gives a concrete, short time box for the beat.

## Conflict

Can the Signal Owner secure agreement for a short pause-and-label on one specific moment without the peer escalating over their head or the tension curdling into a grudge?

## Facilitator notes

- Round 1 (8 min): Peer pushes for an immediate reaction; Signal Owner responds.
- Round 2 (8 min): Signal Owner names the specific trigger (intensity mismatch, call attached, or visible spread) and proposes a time-boxed beat.
- Debrief (10 min): Where did the Signal Owner nearly fold under pressure? What language actually landed with the skeptical peer?
