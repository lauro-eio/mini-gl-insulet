# Case 1 — Naomi and the Changeover Tech Who Went Quiet

## Opening

Naomi supervises a bottling-line changeover crew at a mid-sized packaging plant. She has run changeovers for three years and prides herself on reading a crew fast—who is sharp, who is dragging, who needs a task swapped before it becomes a problem. She rarely feels the need to double-check a first impression.

## Context

Twelve minutes into a hard restart window, Trevor—normally her fastest tech—is unusually quiet, answering questions with single words and avoiding eye contact. Naomi's first read is immediate: he is sulking about being passed over for a lead role last week, and if she calls it out now it will only slow the restart further. She is ready to quietly reassign him to a simpler station for the rest of the shift and deal with the attitude afterward.

A newer tech, Sam, mentions in passing that Trevor seemed fine at the start of shift and only went quiet after checking his phone during the last break.

## Decision point

Naomi can:

1. Act on her read: reassign Trevor to a simpler station now, address what she assumes is attitude in a one-on-one after the shift.
2. Say what she noticed instead of what she concluded, ask Trevor one open question, and wait for his answer before deciding anything about his role for the restart.
3. Say nothing for now, keep Trevor on his current station, and only revisit it if his output actually drops.

## Discussion questions

1. What evidence did Naomi actually have for her read of "sulking about the lead role," versus what she assumed based on the timing?
2. Which of the three conditions—ambiguous signal, a decision riding on the guess, or someone else carrying the consequence—applies most strongly here?
3. If Naomi chooses option 2, what does she say in the first ten seconds, and how does that differ from the reassignment she was ready to make?
