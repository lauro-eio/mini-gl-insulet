# Case 2 — Priya and the Escalating Line 3 Call

## Opening

Priya runs a dispatch control room coordinating eleven delivery drivers for a regional distributor. A customer has been on hold-and-escalate for six minutes over a missed delivery window, and Priya's newest dispatcher, Tomas, is on the call with them. Priya notices Tomas's voice climb half an octave and hears him start to promise a same-day redelivery that she knows is not physically possible given the routes still on the board.

## Context

Priya's first instinct is to grab the headset from Tomas mid-call and take over herself — she can feel the same narrowing in her own chest that she recognizes from her own past hijacks: one move, repeating, "just take the call." She also notices she cannot restate, right now, which two routes actually have capacity this afternoon, even though she reviewed the board twenty minutes ago. That gap is the marker she has learned to catch.

## Decision point

Priya can:

1. Take the headset from Tomas immediately and handle the customer herself, cutting him off mid-sentence in front of the rest of the floor.
2. Mute her own instinct for ten seconds, silently check what she actually knows about today's route capacity, then hand Tomas a specific, accurate line to say instead of taking the call from him.
3. Let Tomas keep going uninterrupted, on the assumption that stepping in at all will undermine him in front of the team, even if the promise he is about to make cannot be kept.

## Discussion questions

1. What marker does Priya notice in herself before she notices anything about Tomas, and how does that change what she does next?
2. If Priya chooses option 2, what does she say to Tomas in the ten seconds she takes, and how is that different from simply taking over the call?
3. How should Priya flag Tomas's own hijack — the climbing voice, the promise he cannot keep — in a way that helps him catch it himself next time rather than just correcting him this time?
