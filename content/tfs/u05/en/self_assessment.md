# Self-Assessment — Is Your Floor Call Framed by Facts or by Fear?

Answer for how you **actually** lead this month (A=1 … D=4). Be honest—this is diagnostic, not a performance review.

--- SECTION 1: QUESTIONS ---

**Q1.** When you announce a call that involves risk (override, hold, mandatory overtime), you usually:
- A) Word it however comes out fastest, without noticing the frame
- B) Notice the frame sometimes, after you've already sent it
- C) Choose the wording deliberately before sending it
- D) Run a quick reframe test—state it the opposite way—before sending it

**Q2.** When a call is naturally worded around avoiding a loss (missed cutoff, lost incentive), you:
- A) Use the loss wording because it gets the fastest agreement
- B) Use it, then wonder afterward if it pushed people toward more risk
- C) Notice the loss framing and consciously check whether it fits the actual stakes
- D) Default to stating the same facts as a protected gain unless the loss frame is truly the honest one

**Q3.** A vivid, recent incident (a near-miss, a bad override) is fresh in your mind while you make a similar call today. You:
- A) Let the recent case set your read on how risky today's situation is
- B) Notice the recent case is influencing you but act on the feeling anyway
- C) Check the recent case against the actual pattern before deciding
- D) Explicitly separate "how it feels because of last time" from "what the data on this actually shows"

**Q4.** A teammate says your framing of a call feels off ("that's not really a guaranteed loss, is it?"). You:
- A) Dismiss it—you worded it that way for a reason
- B) Half-listen, then proceed with the wording you already chose
- C) Pause and restate the facts together to check
- D) Treat it as a formal trigger to run the reframe test before proceeding

**Q5.** Under pressure to get fast buy-in for a risky call, you:
- A) Reach for whichever frame (gain or loss) gets the quickest yes
- B) Notice you're picking the persuasive frame, but do it anyway
- C) Choose the frame that matches the facts, even if it is the slower sell
- D) Explain your framing choice to the team so they can catch it if it drifts

**Q6.** When the same type of risky call comes up repeatedly (a recurring override, a regular overtime ask), you:
- A) Frame each instance fresh, based on how it feels that day
- B) Notice inconsistency sometimes, without a fixed rule
- C) Try to frame it the same honest way each time
- D) Treat it as a standing policy, decided once and applied consistently regardless of the day's mood

**Q7.** When a loss-framed call you made turns out fine, you:
- A) Take it as proof the loss framing was the right call
- B) Feel relieved but do not examine whether it pushed unnecessary risk
- C) Note whether the loss frame matched the actual stakes or just felt urgent
- D) Log whether the frame or the facts drove the outcome, to sharpen the next call

**Q8.** Your team's floor culture around checking a call's framing is:
- A) Nobody names the frame; whoever describes it first sets the tone
- B) It comes up informally, with no shared language
- C) Naming the frame and checking it is respected and expected
- D) The team has a visible ritual (reframe test, log, phrase) that protects the check even under deadline pressure

--- SECTION 2: SCORING ---

Score A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4. Total range 8–32.

--- SECTION 3: PROFILES ---

1. **Frame-Driven Reactor (8–14):** Your announcements run on whichever wording lands fastest, including on risky calls where the frame is quietly pushing the outcome. Pick one recurring risky call and add a ten-second reframe test before you send it.
2. **Occasional Reframer (15–20):** You notice a frame is doing work sometimes, usually after the call is already out. Build one standing rule: run the reframe test before a risk-related announcement, not after.
3. **Deliberate Framer (21–26):** You already choose wording with care and check it against the facts most of the time. Strengthen it by naming your frame out loud so your team learns to flag a drift early.
4. **Policy-Level Framer (27–32):** You run a visible, consistent framing check and treat recurring calls as standing policy rather than fresh judgments. Teach one direct report your reframe test so the ritual survives when you are not on shift.
