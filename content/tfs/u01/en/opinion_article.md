# The Ticket I Almost Closed by Instinct

I have closed thousands of tickets by feel. Most of the time feel is right—that is what years on this floor bought me. But last month feel almost cost us a recall.

A pallet came through with a label mismatch, the kind we see weekly, always the same fix: repull, relabel, move on. My hand was on the "resolved" button in maybe ten seconds. Then Priya, who had been on the line two months, said, "Wait, this SKU is on the hold list from Tuesday's memo."

I did not remember a hold list. My gut said "same problem, same fix" because the pattern looked identical. It was not. The batch carried a supplier defect flag that only showed up if you opened the second tab in the tracking system, invisible on the label itself.

That is the moment I started paying attention to which speed I am running on. Most of my calls are the fast kind, and they should stay fast. I have made this exact call correctly hundreds of times, and slowing every one down would just make me the bottleneck everyone waits on. But a few calls a week do not match anything I have truly practiced, or the cost of being wrong is high, or something in front of me quietly disagrees with my gut. Those are the calls where fast is a liability, not a skill.

Now, before I resolve anything unfamiliar or costly, I ask myself one question: have I actually handled this exact pattern before, or does it just look like something I have handled before? If the honest answer is "it just looks similar," I take the extra ninety seconds the slow pass requires: the second tab, the hold list, the shift log, whatever applies.

Priya's catch did not feel like a compliment at the time. It felt like she had caught me being careless. She had not. She had caught me being fast on a call that needed slow. I told the team that afternoon: the goal is not to double-check everything, that would grind the floor to a halt. The goal is to notice the handful of moments each shift where instinct is guessing, not remembering, and to buy the ninety seconds before the button, not after.
