# Strategic Role-Play — Protecting the Check Ritual Under Deadline Pressure

**Duration:** 40–50 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice securing buy-in for a protected pause-and-check ritual when multiple stakeholders want the floor to keep moving at full speed.

## Four roles

1. **Supervisor (protagonist):** Wants approval to run a standing rule: any call with novelty, high stakes, or conflicting signals gets a short, time-boxed check before action.
2. **Speed-focused ops manager (antagonist):** Measured on throughput and cutoff times; sees any pause as lost minutes and calls the ritual "second-guessing the team."
3. **Quality or safety analyst (skeptic):** Supports the idea in principle but needs named criteria for when the check applies, plus a clear time box so it cannot be used to stall indefinitely.
4. **Peer supervisor (ally or swing):** Will support the ritual if it does not slow down calls that genuinely do not need it, and if it does not quietly shift extra work onto neighboring shifts.

## Conflict thesis

Can the supervisor protect a named, time-boxed check for genuinely risky calls without looking slow to the ops manager or vague to the analyst?

## Success look-fors

- Named triggers (novelty, stakes, conflicting signals), not a blanket "when in doubt, pause"
- A fixed time box for the check, agreed in advance
- A visible record of when the check was used and what it caught or ruled out
- No secret second process that contradicts the agreed ritual under pressure

## Debrief prompts

1. Which argument landed with the ops manager versus the analyst?
2. How did the supervisor turn "pause" into language that reads as risk control, not hesitation?
3. What would cause the ritual to quietly disappear during the next crunch week—and who owns preventing that?
