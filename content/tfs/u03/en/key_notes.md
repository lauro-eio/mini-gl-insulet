# Key Notes — Reading Numbers Without Fooling Yourself

**Unit goal:** Read floor numbers without over-trusting small samples, expect a pull-back after an outlier, weigh base rates against tidy causal stories, and let a checked rule run recurring calls.

## Core ideas

- **Small samples swing hard:** a handful of observations can produce a dramatic-looking rate purely by chance—check the count before reacting to the number.
- **Regression to the mean:** an unusually good or bad result tends to be followed by a result closer to average, with no cause required.
- **Base rate before story:** a plausible cause-and-effect explanation can overwhelm the plainer explanation that this swing happens anyway some of the time.
- **Formula beats gut on repeats:** for decisions you make often, a simple, consistently applied rule outperforms case-by-case intuition; override it only for a genuinely decisive new fact.

## Frontline leader checklist

1. Before reacting to a defect rate, output swing, or attendance spike, ask how many observations it is based on.
2. After a record-setting good or bad result, expect the next result to land closer to normal—do not assign a cause to a swing regression alone predicts.
3. Ask what the base rate for this kind of swing is before accepting the first causal story offered.
4. For calls you make repeatedly, write the rule down and follow it by default; log any override with the specific reason.
5. Protect the sample-size question when a teammate raises it, even under deadline pressure—it is a check, not a delay.

## Reflection

Which recent "great week" or "bad week" on your floor did you explain with a story, when a base rate or a small sample might explain it just as well?
