# Strategic Role-Play — Protecting the Numbers-Check Ritual Under Pressure

**Duration:** 40–50 minutes
**Purpose:** Practice securing buy-in for a standing baseline-and-sample check when multiple stakeholders want a fast verdict on every striking number.

## Four roles

1. **Supervisor (protagonist):** Wants approval for a standing rule: any number that is a small sample, a record result, or paired with a ready-made causal story gets a short, time-boxed baseline check before it drives a decision or a plant-wide rollout.
2. **Speed-focused ops manager (antagonist):** Measured on throughput and ship schedules; sees any pause on a numbers-based call as lost time and calls the ritual "overthinking a simple number."
3. **Quality or data analyst (skeptic):** Supports the idea in principle but needs named triggers for when the check applies, plus a fixed time box, so it cannot be used to stall every decision indefinitely.
4. **Peer supervisor (ally or swing):** Will support the ritual if it does not slow down numbers that genuinely do not need a check, and if it does not get used to quietly dismiss inconvenient results.

## Conflict thesis

Can the supervisor protect a named, time-boxed baseline check for genuinely ambiguous numbers without looking slow to the ops manager or vague to the analyst?

## Success look-fors

- Named triggers (small sample, record result, ready-made story), not a blanket "let's not trust any number"
- A fixed time box for the check, agreed in advance
- A visible record of when the check was used and whether it confirmed a real shift or a pull-back to normal
- No quiet exception where an inconvenient result skips the check while a convenient one does not

## Debrief prompts

1. Which argument landed with the ops manager versus the analyst?
2. How did the supervisor turn "check the baseline" into language that reads as risk control, not slowness?
3. What would cause the ritual to quietly disappear during the next record week or crisis—and who owns preventing that?
