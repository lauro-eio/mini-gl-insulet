# Case 2 — Diego's Best Week and the Slump After

## Opening

Diego supervises a nine-person packing team. Three weeks ago, he introduced a five-minute pre-shift huddle where the team calls out yesterday's near-misses before starting. That same week, the team posted its best output-and-accuracy week on record. Diego told the plant manager the huddle was the reason and pushed to make it standard across all four packing teams starting immediately.

## Context

The plant manager approved the rollout. One week later, Diego's own team's numbers came back down to their normal range—still solid, but nowhere near the record week. Two of the other three teams that adopted the huddle saw no change at all; one saw a slight dip. Diego's team's weekly output-and-accuracy composite score for the past six weeks looks like this:

| Week | Composite score | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 (before huddle) | 71, 74, 69, 73 | Normal range |
| 5 (huddle introduced) | 89 | Record week |
| 6 (huddle continues) | 72 | Back to normal range |

The plant manager now wants a straight answer in tomorrow's ops review: does the huddle work, and should the other teams keep running it?

## Decision point

Diego can:

1. Defend the huddle as the proven cause of week 5's record score, since it is the only thing that changed that week.
2. Tell the plant manager the single record week does not settle the question, and propose tracking the composite score across all four teams for eight more weeks before drawing a conclusion.
3. Quietly keep running the huddle on his own team regardless of the data, since it costs little, while staying vague with the plant manager about whether it explains the record week.

## Discussion questions

1. What does the six-week table suggest about how much of week 5's record score the huddle can actually explain, versus ordinary week-to-week variation?
2. If Diego picks option 1, what is he likely to face when next month's numbers normalize further, or when another team's data disagrees?
3. What would a fair test of "does the huddle work" look like, given that Diego cannot run his team twice under identical conditions?
