# Case 1 — The Shipment Save That Wasn't What We Remembered

## Opening

Renata supervises a receiving dock crew of twelve. Every Friday she runs a fifteen-minute debrief where the crew retells the week's hardest moment, mostly to build morale and catch anything worth flagging upward. The stories are usually close enough to what happened that nobody has ever felt the need to check them against the log.

## Context

This week's story is about a mislabeled pallet that nearly shipped to the wrong distribution center. Two senior leads tell it the same way: they spotted the label mismatch during a routine walk, held the pallet, and caught it with two hours to spare before the truck's cutoff. It is a clean story, and the crew is proud of it.

Renata pulls the dock log before finalizing the weekly report, mostly out of habit. The timestamps show something different: the mismatch was actually flagged by a scanner alert forty minutes before either lead walked past the pallet, and the alert was first acknowledged by a part-time loader who has been on the team six weeks. The two-hour cushion the leads remember was closer to forty minutes by the time the hold was actually confirmed.

Nobody invented anything. The leads genuinely remember catching it themselves, because their walk-past is the last and most vivid part of the sequence they personally witnessed — the part that became the story's peak. The scanner alert and the loader's acknowledgment happened earlier and less dramatically, and neither made it into the retelling.

## Decision point

Renata can:

1. Let this week's story stand as told — correcting it would embarrass two respected leads over a pallet that got caught either way.
2. Quietly fix the written weekly report to match the log, without raising it in front of the crew, and say nothing about the gap between story and record.
3. Bring the timestamps into next week's debrief before any story is told, credit the loader by name, and use the moment to reset how the team builds its after-action stories going forward.

## Discussion questions

1. What made the leads' account feel completely true to them, even though the log shows a different sequence?
2. Which distortion is most at work here — hindsight, outcome bias, or peak-end memory — and how can you tell?
3. If Renata chooses option 3, how should she raise the gap without making the two leads defensive or the loader uncomfortable?
