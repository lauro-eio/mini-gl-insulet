# The Debrief Where Our Story Didn't Match the Log

I have run the Friday debrief the same way for years: gather the crew, ask "what happened this week," let the clearest voice tell the story, nod, move on. Last Friday the story was a good one — maybe too good.

Marcus told it like this: the line 3 belt started slipping around 2 p.m., he caught the smell before anyone flagged it, called it in himself, and had a spare part on the way before the line even stopped. Everyone nodded. It was the kind of story that makes a shift feel like it was under control the whole time.

Then Dana, who keeps the shift log because nobody else wants to, read the timestamps out loud. The belt alarm fired at 1:41. Marcus's first call to maintenance was logged at 2:09 — twenty-eight minutes later, not "right away." And the person who actually flagged the smell first was a temp worker whose name nobody in the room could remember.

Nobody was lying. Marcus genuinely remembered it the way he told it. The ending — the part rescued in time — was so clear in everyone's mind that it quietly edited the messier middle: the twenty-eight minutes, the temp worker's catch, the fact that the line partially stalled before the fix landed. The story was coherent. The log was accurate. They were not the same thing.

I used to think a good debrief was one where the room agreed on a clean version of events fast. Now I think that speed is the warning sign. If everyone's story lines up perfectly and effortlessly, we have probably already smoothed over the parts that would have been useful to examine — who actually caught it, how long it really took, what we did not know until it was over.

So the debrief runs differently now. Before anyone tells the story, Dana reads the timestamps first. Only after the room has the raw sequence do we build the narrative — and we build it to match the log, not the other way around. It takes four extra minutes. It has already changed two conversations about who gets credit, and surfaced one gap we would have missed entirely if the first telling had stood unchallenged.
